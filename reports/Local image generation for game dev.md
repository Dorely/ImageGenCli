# Keep the 4070: licenses outrank VRAM

**This PC is feasible for local game art, and no hardware purchase is justified today.** The strongest local models are Qwen-Image-2.1, FLUX 3 Image Klein and Fibo 2.0, effectively tied at the top of the Artificial Analysis (AA) open-weights board. Only FLUX 3 Klein can ship in a commercial game without a separate license deal, and only while revenue stays under $5M. It also has the only vendor-measured RTX 4070 figure found: 8.9 s at 1024². Every local model still scores worse than gpt-image-2 on overall preference, by 143–155 rating points at best, which implies raters choose gpt-image-2 about 70% of the time. Local wins where leaderboards do not look: cost per image, fixed seeds, trained styles, and alpha, although the best native-alpha model is not shippable. gpt-image-2 itself has been superseded by OpenAI's GPT Image 2.5 and 2.6, so "same as gpt-image-2" does not mean frontier. The upgrade answer is $0 now; if volume later forces a purchase, a used 24 GB RTX 3090 at about $1,300–$1,570 comes first, and the full answer to "how big" is about $2,200–$2,650 with 64 GB of RAM. All asset-level verdicts below are judgment, because no controlled game-asset benchmark exists and no model has been run on this PC.

## No local model beats gpt-image-2 overall, yet several beat it on control

**gpt-image-2 is now a superseded yardstick.** It launched April 21, 2026, and OpenAI has since shipped GPT Image 2.5 (September 8) and 2.6 (November 4) at the same token rates ([changelog](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/changelog); [pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing)). It is not deprecated ([deprecations](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/deprecations)), and it stays the baseline here because you asked for it and because it has a pinned snapshot and settled scores. But 2.5 Sunburst now leads Arena at 1427 against 1382 on text-to-image and 1517 against 1460 on edit, both preliminary ([Arena text-to-image](https://arena.ai/leaderboard/text-to-image); [Arena edit](https://arena.ai/leaderboard/image-edit)). Against that ceiling the best local gap on Arena text-to-image widens from 155 points to 200. Tier names also moved: 2.5 `high` reportedly costs what gpt-image-2 `medium` costs, $0.053 at 1024² ([Kanaries](https://docs.kanaries.net/articles/gpt-image-2-5)).

**The best local generation is a three-way tie that licensing breaks.** AA's open-weights board shows Qwen-Image-2.1 at 1045, FLUX 3 Klein at 1043 and Fibo 2.0 at 1039, all inside the ±12 interval shown for first place ([AA open weights](https://artificialanalysis.ai/image/leaderboard/text-to-image/open-weights)). Qwen is research-only and Fibo is CC BY-NC, which leaves FLUX 3 Klein as the one a solo developer can ship without a separate deal. The gap to gpt-image-2 is real. Under a standard Elo curve, 143–155 points means raters prefer gpt-image-2 about 70% of the time, and the 51–96 point edit gap means about 57–64% (my calculation, assuming both boards follow that scale). Hand-graded tests agree: GenAI Showdown gives gpt-image-2 12 of 15 adversarial prompts against 8 for the best open model, and passes took it 1–2 attempts where open models sometimes needed 18 ([GenAI Showdown](https://genai-showdown.specr.net/)). Its edit board reads 10 of 12 against 7 ([edit board](https://genai-showdown.specr.net/image-editing)). Kingy's test scored 30 of 32 against 24 of 32 for FLUX 3 Klein, with the prompt upsampler off and an AI judge from the same vendor as the OpenAI configurations ([Kingy](https://kingy.ai/blog/flux-3-image-klein-tested-benchmarks-costs/)).

**Treat 8.9 s as a floor.** FLUX 3 Klein's card warns that short prompts sent as typed come out generic, and Comfy's templates enable the prompt upsampler by default ([card](https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/flux-3-image-klein); [Comfy docs](https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/flux/flux-3-klein)). That upsampler is a 9.75 GB int8 or 6.72 GB w4a8 file ([files](https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/flux-3-image-klein-comfy)) and cost about 17 s on an RTX 3080 ([Hausmer](https://hausmer.com/posts/flux-3-klein-vs-qwen-image/)). Expect roughly 26 s per image with it on (extrapolated), or write long prompts yourself and test the difference. The low-memory path also depends on ComfyUI, because BFL's reference code peaks at 26–31 GB and has no int8 route ([Xenospectrum](https://xenospectrum.com/en/flux-klein-local-image-generation/)).

**System RAM limits only the heavy models.** FLUX.2 dev's FP8 transformer and text encoder total 53.5 GB ([Comfy-Org files](https://huggingface.co/Comfy-Org/flux2-dev)), far past what 32 GB can cache. ComfyUI's Dynamic VRAM loads weights as file-backed memory that Windows can reclaim and re-read, so the likely result at 32 GB is slowdowns and disk re-reads rather than crashes ([Comfy](https://blog.comfy.org/p/dynamic-vram-in-comfyui-saving-local)). That is reasoning, since no firsthand test of 12 GB plus 32 GB on FLUX.2 dev was found. The nearest signal is an RTX 3060 host with 15 GB of RAM against one with about 150 GB: 159.8 s against 68.3 s for the first FLUX.1 image, then 65.15 s against 63.91 s once warm, with a slower PCIe slot confounding the result ([ComputingForGeeks](https://computingforgeeks.com/best-gpu-for-stable-diffusion-comfyui/)). Ignore the widely repeated figure of 60–80 s for FLUX.2 dev on 12 GB, because its ranges match a table that InsiderLLM labels FLUX.1 ([Botmonster](https://botmonster.com/ai/how-to-set-up-flux-2-dev-locally-in-2026/); [InsiderLLM](https://insiderllm.com/guides/flux-locally-complete-guide/)).

## Every upgrade buys speed or restricted models, not better shippable art

**The justified upgrade today is $0.** On AA, no open model that needs more memory than this PC has ranks above FLUX 3 Klein. Only Qwen-Image-2.1 sits higher, by 2 points inside the margin, and it runs quantized on 12 GB. A 5090 produces the same model's images 2.2× faster at 1 MP and 3.4× faster at 2048², not better ones.

The honest case for more memory is fidelity and the Apache-licensed Qwen family. Leaderboards do not test this PC's int8 builds. One tester found BF16 clearly better than FP8 for Qwen editing ([MyAIForce](https://myaiforce.com/qwen-image-2-1/)), and Comfy says bf16 follows long prompts more closely ([Comfy docs](https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/flux/flux-3-klein)). Nobody has measured that loss on FLUX 3 Klein. More memory would also move Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 from overnight to usable and make LoRA training less fragile.

The break-even figures are arithmetic from the research notes, assume $0.001 per image in running cost, and ignore quality. They also flatter upgrades, because the 4070 already makes FLUX 3 Klein images for almost nothing, so a new card only displaces work this one cannot do. Take a 300-card set at 20 candidates per card, an illustrative size: 6,000 generations cost $318 at gpt-image-2 medium or $1,266 at high, against under $1 of electricity and about 15 machine-hours here at 8.9 s. That whole set does not repay even a 5070 Ti against high-tier pricing. At 1,000 images a month against medium, $1,400 takes about 27 months and $6,919 about 11 years.

**Buy only on a trigger.** The first is sustained volume above about 2,000 images a month on models that do not fit. The second is a recurring need for Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 or LoRA training that rented time cannot cover. Then buy capacity rather than speed: the fitting models are already fast, so the 3090's 24 GB is the point of the purchase, and the 5070 Ti adds only 4 GB plus a warranty. Rent first, since RTX 4090 time has a [median of $0.49/h](https://getdeploying.com/gpus/nvidia-rtx-4090) and a Klein LoRA takes [about an hour on a 4090](https://huggingface.co/blog/black-forest-labs/flux-2-klein-lora). That is about $0.50–$1.50 of utilized time per run allowing up to three hours (my arithmetic, excluding setup). For heavy one-offs, fal lists [Qwen-Image at $0.02/MP](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/qwen-image/llms.txt). Nothing consumer holds FLUX.2 dev FP8 fully in VRAM, because the ~35.1 GB estimate needs a 48 GB pro card.

If you do buy, three details matter. Choose one matched two-stick RAM kit: official Ryzen 9000 speed falls from 5600 to 3600 with four sticks ([Newegg community](https://www.newegg.com/gamer/community/t/why-is-ram-compatibility-still-an-issue-on-ryzen/3409)), and 2×48 GB is reported as no harder than 2×32 GB ([Level1Techs](https://forum.level1techs.com/t/ddr5-4-dimms-on-am5-whats-working-whats-not/197153), forum anecdote). Read the PSU label first, since its wattage is unknown: one guide lists minimum and comfort sizes of 750 W and 850 W for the 5070 Ti and 1000 W and 1200 W for the 5090, with 750–1000 W units at $95–$112 in October ([DropReference](https://dropreference.com/en/blog/guide/power-supply-rtx-5070-5070-ti-5080-5090-2026-wattage-atx-31)). No figure was sourced for used 24 GB cards, so assume 850 W until you check the specific card. And expect no platform bottleneck: a 5090 on PCIe 4.0 x16 showed almost no loss in Puget's Resolve testing, though that is not an image-generation test ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/motherboards/lack-of-pcie-bandwidth-can-nerf-rtx-5090-by-up-to-25-percent-in-content-creation-workloads-puget-data-confirms-performance-hit-when-using-older-generations-and-fewer-lanes)).

Waiting has little cost, although prices are still climbing ([Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/nvidia-rtx-50-gpus-see-a-massive-price-increase-in-november/)). The RTX 50 Super line is unannounced, with [one report](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/gpus/report-claims-nvidia-will-not-be-releasing-any-new-rtx-gaming-gpus-in-2026-rtx-60-series-likely-debuting-in-2028) of no new gaming cards in 2026. A [rumored](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Nvidia-RTX-50-Super-series-launch-tipped-once-again-later-than-expected.1317926.0.html) 24 GB 5070 Ti Super at a sane price would reorder this table.

| **Icons and UI** | Style LoRA, one icon per generation, shared palette pass | Slightly worse: one-call batches work on the cloud side (vendor evidence) | Moderate; align by hand |
| **In-image text, logos** | Set in the engine or with a font; Z-Image or Qwen-Image-2512 for short titles only | Worse, though OpenAI also admits placement and clarity limits | High if text must be exact |
| **Seamless textures, PBR** | Tiling nodes on SD-class models, or a [post-process tiler](https://github.com/gokayfem/ComfyUI-Texture-Simple) with a 2×2 proof | Little evidence either way; local is usable | Check in engine; roughness is estimated |
| **Autotile tilesets** | No proven route; generate terrain reference, hand-build the template | Same: both poor | High |
| **Backgrounds** | Any current model; Qwen-Image-Layered for parallax separation | Same on simple scenes; worse on complex layouts (no direct test) | Low to moderate |

Pixel art shows how post-processing levels the field. gpt-image-2 cannot generate below 655,360 pixels, which makes 816×816 the smallest square ([OpenAI guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation?model=gpt-image-2)). One vendor's 32×32 request returned 44,725 unique colors with about 2% of color changes on the true grid, which conversion fixed to 12 colors and full alignment ([Rangy](https://rangy.ai/blog/ai-pixel-art/), one image). Local output needs the same pass. Animation stays poor on both sides: practitioners report misaligned frames and illogical walk cycles from gpt-image-2 ([r/aigamedev snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1ss9bjz/snapshots/2026-04-25T00%3A45%3A58.465245Z)), and the best local spritesheet LoRA's author lists cut-off hair and an inconsistent back-view row ([svntax-dev](https://huggingface.co/svntax-dev/pixel_spritesheet_4walk_small_lora_v1)). A hosted specialist such as PixelLab or Retro Diffusion is worth a look if a project needs many directional sprites, though no independent test confirms it beats either route ([Skaldborn](https://www.skaldborn.com/devlog/05-comfyui-setup/); [Ludo](https://ludo.ai/compare/best-ai-pixel-art-generators)).

**For the card game, generate frameless art and keep frames, costs, names and rules text as engine layers.** That sidesteps the text weakness that both OpenAI and BFL document. It also adds human-authored arrangement, which matters because US guidance treats prompt-only output as unprotected, while *Zarya of the Dawn* kept protection for its human text and arrangement ([Promise Legal](https://blog.promise.legal/ai-game-assets-copyright-steam-disclosure-2026/)). Card art is also where audience risk concentrates. Steam requires disclosure of AI content that ships and that players consume ([Steamworks](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey)), and disclosing games average 76.4% positive reviews against 81.5% for others, a correlation that may partly reflect low-effort games ([Sulka Haro](https://fragwyz.substack.com/p/half-of-new-steam-games-now-disclose)). Vapor World removed AI cutscenes after its character visibly shifted between shots ([IndieForGames](https://indieforgames.com/steam-ai-art-backlash/)). Budget consistency QA and paint-overs for the art players look at most, or treat AI as placeholder art and replace the hero cards later. [Deck Beasts](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5004310/Deck_Beasts/) discloses AI use for placeholder card art, and [Beyond Words](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyond_Words_%28video_game%29) replaced its early AI art after criticism.

## Build the pipeline around ComfyUI, FLUX 3 Klein and a license manifest

Start with the free fixes. Read the PSU label. Move the display cable to the motherboard's video output, which should return about 2.8 GB of VRAM, after confirming the 9700X's integrated graphics work on this board, since the AMD spec page could not be retrieved. Try manual DDR5-6000 at 1:1 rather than the 6600 EXPO profile, because the default ratio becomes 1:2 above 6000 ([HWBusters](https://hwbusters.com/cpu/ddr5-6000-vs-8000-mhz-ram-amd-ryzen-9-9950x/2/)), then run a memory test. Expect little change in sampling speed, since both RAM speeds exceed the PCIe link (my reasoning, unmeasured). Keep a system-managed pagefile ([Popular AI](https://www.popularai.org/p/how-much-ram-for-comfyui)) and store models on the NVMe drive, because Dynamic VRAM re-reads evicted weights from disk.

Windows 10 is not a blocker. Your driver, 621.01, appears to be the last Windows 10 Game Ready release, with quarterly security drivers following through October 2029 ([Gazlog](https://gazlog.jp/entry/geforce-game-ready-driver-621-01-whql/); [TechRadar](https://techradar.com/computing/gpu/time-for-gamers-to-upgrade-to-windows-11-nvidia-gpu-driver-support-for-windows-10-ends-in-two-months-heres-what-you-need-to-know)). No NVIDIA page confirming that was found. ComfyUI still supports Windows 10 ([Comfy docs](https://docs.comfy.org/installation/system_requirements)), and consumer security updates run to October 12, 2027 if you are enrolled ([Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/extended-security-updates)). The future risk is a PyTorch or CUDA build expecting a newer driver, which pinned installs reduce.

Run it as a job system rather than a chat window. Export API-format workflows into version control, drive them from a Python runner, and write a manifest per asset with seed, model hash, LoRA, prompt and license, so that one non-commercial node cannot silently taint a batch. Upscalers need the same check, since 4x-UltraSharp is listed as CC-BY-NC-SA ([compilation](https://huggingface.co/easygoing0114/AI_upscalers)), and CHORD's tileable PBR is research-only ([Comfy](https://blog.comfy.org/p/ubisoft-open-sources-the-chord-model)). Pin ComfyUI, node commits and model hashes per game, because new-model support still breaks ([ComfyUI Models](https://comfyuimodels.com/qwen-image-2-1/low-vram)). Try native formats (int8, FP8, NVFP4) before GGUF.

For LoRAs, train only on your own, commissioned, CC0 or properly licensed art, and keep the dataset list. Do not train on FLUX 3 Klein outputs: §4.b of its license bars using outputs to improve similar models ([license](https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/flux-3-image-klein/blob/main/LICENSE.md)), and a LoRA dataset is at risk under that clause (my reading). Plan on 20–50 distinct images for one character ([Airmore](https://airmore.ai/lora-training-guide.html)). Try one Klein 4B run locally, expecting 1–3+ hours (extrapolated), and rent a 24 GB card if it is too slow or unstable. The finished LoRA should run at home either way. Reach for the cloud on layout-heavy single sheets, hard compositions and one-off hero images, where gpt-image-2-class models need far fewer attempts. OpenAI requires organization verification first ([guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation)), and because 2.5 tiers do not map to gpt-image-2 tiers, compare by price rather than by name.

## An $8 test settles the two biggest open questions

**The transparency conflict is unresolved at primary-source level, and this report scores gpt-image-2 as having no usable native alpha.** The reports fit a timeline: none at launch ([Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ai/models/openai/gpt-image-2/)), a preview from August 20 ([changelog](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/changelog)), and a reported end around September 30 ([APIYI](https://docs.apiyi.com/en/live/2026-10/gpt-image-2-transparent-shutdown.md), a reseller). From October 1, developers report HTTP 400 with the message "Transparent background is not supported for this model." ([OpenAI forum](https://community.openai.com/t/is-transparent-background-support-broken-in-gpt-image-2/1402707)), and APIYI's October 15 run saw all 14 generation and 7 edit calls rejected and unbilled. Yet on November 6 the [API reference](https://developers.openai.com/api/reference/resources/images/methods/generate) still says preview, no OpenAI statement ending it was found, and no OpenAI staff member confirmed it in the thread. A separate vendor probe, from an unstated date, got real RGBA on one route and plain RGB on fal, with output capped near 1.5K and contaminated soft edges ([ChatImg](https://chatimg.ai/en/blog/gpt-image-2-transparent-background)). During the preview, one developer also found the flag pushed output toward photorealism ([OpenAI forum](https://community.openai.com/t/does-background-transparent-also-change-the-rendering-style-of-gpt-image-2/1392632)). If alpha returns, Qwen-2.1's and Layered's "Better" falls to "Same". GPT Image 2.5 already supports it, so against 2.5 local is "Same" at best. Whether 2.6 does was not confirmed.

**The largest gap is the absence of a controlled game-asset benchmark.** No independent test compares gpt-image-2 with any open model on sprites, tilesets, icons or character sheets, none includes a LoRA-tuned model, and neither leaderboard has a game-asset category. Votes reward general appeal. A style-locked local model could beat untuned gpt-image-2 on set consistency, and nothing here proves or disproves that. Other gaps are firsthand 4070 timings for FLUX.2 dev, Qwen and Z-Image, a real 12 GB LoRA timing, edge-quality comparison of BiRefNet, Qwen RGBA and OpenAI alpha, Arena scores for FLUX 3 Klein and Fibo 2.0, reliable data on fantasy-violence refusals, 2.6 per-image pricing, OpenAI's output-ownership terms, and any shipped-game post-mortem with measured cleanup hours. Most pages were read through an automated summarizer, OpenAI's launch post returned HTTP 403, and Reddit was barely reachable. None of this is legal advice.

You can close the two biggest gaps yourself in an evening. Send one `background: "transparent"` request, since rejected calls were reportedly unbilled. Then take 30 fixed prompts spanning card art, icons, cutouts and pixel stills, and run them on gpt-image-2 high ($6.33) and GPT Image 2.5 `high` ($1.59 by Kanaries' figure), for about $8 plus any input tokens. Run the same prompts on FLUX 3 Klein and Klein 4B, with each template run twice and the second time recorded, as [LocalAIMaster suggests](https://localaimaster.com/blog/best-gpu-for-image-generation). Score accepted assets per 20 candidates and minutes of cleanup per accepted asset, because those two numbers decide this choice better than any rating.

## Conclusion

The instinct behind the question, that better local art waits behind a bigger GPU, no longer matches the market. Quantization has brought the top of the open-weight board within reach of a 12 GB card, licenses decide what a commercial game can use, and GPU prices have pushed break-even beyond one game's asset count. The scarce resource is now review labor. gpt-image-2's 1–2 attempts against as many as 18 is a gap no local hardware closes, because a faster card only produces rejects sooner. That points to a split by asset rather than one winner: local for style-locked volume, a current OpenAI model for the few hard compositions, and a human for text, animation and hero cards.

Open weights also carry a risk that hardware does not solve. The top tier turned over in eight weeks, Qwen moved from Apache at 2512 to a research license at 2.1 ([Cellcog](https://cellcog.ai/blog/qwen-image-2-1/)), and Microsoft's official Mage-Flow repo returned 404 about a week after launch ([report](https://0dev.duckdns.org/en/posts/microsoft-removes-mage-flow-models-from-hugging-face)). Archive the exact weights and license text with each project, and keep the pipeline model-agnostic. Revisit the upgrade when a 24 GB card ships at a sane price, when BFL clarifies FLUX 3 Klein LoRA rights, or when your own test shows int8 costing visible quality.
